SUPERVISORY COMMITTEE
Meet the Supervisory Committee
Appointed by the Board of Directors, the Supervisory Committee provides independent oversight on behalf of members. The Committee is part of the credit union’s governance structure — independent from day-to-day management and accountable to members.
Members must belong to the credit union, be bondable, and serve the same Board-established term length of one, two, or three years.

SUPERVISORY COMMITTEE
What We Do
Our work really falls into four areas. We monitor credit union activity, check the safeguards, oversee the independent audit process, and monitor corrective action.
Monitor Credit Union Activity
Board and Credit Committee minutes

Financial information and selected reports

Unusual activity, exceptions, or concerns
Check the Safeguards
Are policies and procedures being followed?

Are appropriate internal controls in place?

Are member assets, accounts, and information protected?
Oversee the Independent Audit
Select the independent CPA and arrange the required audit

Review CPA and NCUA findings

Ask questions and request more review when needed
Monitor Corrective Action
Recommend action to the Board

Monitor whether issues are addressed

Keep records and report to the Board
This is how the Committee protects member interests: we ask questions, check that safeguards are working, and make sure issues receive the attention they need.

SUPERVISORY COMMITTEE
The Three-Part Oversight Structure
The Supervisory Committee is one part of the credit union’s broader independent and external oversight structure. Each part has a distinct role.
National Credit Union Administration (NCUA)
Provides federal regulatory examination and oversight of the credit union's safety, soundness, operations, and compliance.
Yount, Hyde & Barbour, P.C. — Independent CPA Firm
Performs the detailed agreed-upon procedures — testing financial records, accounts, controls, and other required areas — and reports the findings.
NSLFCU Supervisory Committee
Appointed by the Board. Oversees the audit process, reviews results, follows up on findings, recommends action, and reports to the Board.
Combined effect: regulatory supervision + independent testing + member-focused oversight.
